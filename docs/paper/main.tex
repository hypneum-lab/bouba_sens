\begin{abstract}
We introduce \texttt{bouba\_sens}, a pre-registered benchmark
measuring three invariants of cross-modal plasticity --- B-1
(congenital-blindness T1/T2 gap, \citealp{amedi2007}), B-2
(informational migration under lesion, Me3 delta), and B-3
(perceptive/proprioceptive structural asymmetry, bouba/kiki) ---
across five worlds (Gaussian, XOR, Sinusoid, Studyforrest mock,
real MIT-BIH ECG) and \textbf{two real bridges with opposite
real/synthetic modality splits}. The Studyforrest bridge (OpenNeuro
ds000113) carries real force, gravity, and tactile/label channels,
while audio and vision are annotation-derived proxies. The CC-film
bridge (Big Buck Bunny, CC-BY 3.0) inverts this: audio (log-mel),
vision (frames), and labels (shot-cuts) are real perceptual signal,
while tactile, gravity, and force are synthetic (a film has no
proprioception).
Thresholds $(0.05, 0.10, 0.02)$ were frozen in a git-dated
design specification (2026-04-20) before the evaluation campaign;
no bouba\_sens hypothesis is registered on OSF. Across 9 grids
(150 cells each, 5 seeds $\times$ 5
modalities $\times$ 2 timings $\times$ 3 SNR labels; the three SNR
labels were near-replicates, see ADR-0020) and 19 ADRs, we
report: (i) B-3 passes on magnitude only (median max off-diagonal
$= 0.109$--$0.125$, $5.5$--$6.3\times$ threshold, 5/5 seeds); a
null-model partition control places the pre-registered
perceptive/proprioceptive partition at the 44--72th percentile of
random alternatives, and the magnitude tracks per-modality
entropy, so the original ``architectural property'' reading of
B-3 is withdrawn (ADR-0014, ADR-0015); the $0.02$ threshold itself
was never compared with a null, and an independent binomial null at
the production probe size invalidates the reading of the bare
exceedance of $0.02$, so no compatibility or rejection verdict on
B-3 is established before a paired calibration of the median (same
probe for the five queries, shared seeds and worlds; ADR-0021) ---
B-3 is reported as an uncalibrated magnitude; with a corrected
linear probe and a preregistered paired block null, B-3 v2 rejects
symmetry, but this is an asymmetry built into the worlds (unequally
informative modalities), not a lesion effect: the preregistered
criterion is met, H0 was ill-posed, and the residual after removing
the query-modality effect is at the null level (ADR-0023);
(ii) B-1 does not survive multi-seed validation --- the
single-seed $+0.0125$ peak (Sprint 10) inverts to $-0.0250$ on
the canonical seed and yields a 10-seed sign-test $p=0.62$
(chance); pre-registered subgroup replication on $N=10$ new seeds
fails at Bonferroni $\alpha=0.025$ (ADR-0019) --- B-1 is below
the noise floor; (iii) B-2 does not exceed threshold under any
configuration (ADR-0019). The earlier ``Kraskov and MINE agree at
zero'' reading is withdrawn as an independent agreement, because the
1-D mean-pooled probe pinned MINE to exactly $0.000$ on every seed;
re-estimating with Kraskov, adaptive binning, and MINE on the full
896-D probe, with a measured effective dimension $D \approx 12$
replacing the ``probe batch $\geq 128$'' heuristic by an
estimator-aware $N \gtrsim f(D)$ criterion, no estimator crosses
$+0.10$ on any seed on the Gaussian reference or on either real
bridge (Section~\ref{sec:limitations-e1e2}). An earlier version of this paper
claimed that a frozen mux with hard transducer gating is the
minimal configuration that qualitatively reproduces the Amedi
T1/T2 asymmetry; that claim rested on the single-seed B-1 pattern
and is withdrawn together with it. \texttt{bouba\_sens} is released at
\url{https://git.saillant.cc/hypneum-lab/bouba_sens}
(tag \texttt{v0.5.9}, ADRs 0001--0021) as a reusable instrument for
stress-testing cross-modal architectures under git-dated
pre-registration; the B-1 retraction (ADR-0019) constitutes a
portable multi-seed validation protocol for future benchmarks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

Two independent lines of evidence suggest that cross-modal
asymmetry is a \emph{structural} property of cognitive
architectures. \citet{amedi2007} report that, in congenital
blindness, occipital cortex is re-tasked to audition with
\emph{greater} plasticity than in late-acquired blindness ---
a quantitative T1/T2 gap that critical-period theories
\citep{bavelier2002cross, merabet2010neural} attribute to a
lock-style plasticity constraint mid-development. The bouba/kiki
effect \citep{kohler1947gestalt, ramachandran2001synaesthesia}
independently reports $\geq 95\,\%$ cross-cultural agreement on a
round-vs-spiky sound-shape mapping, evidence of a hard-wired
\emph{asymmetric} modality mapping \citep{sidhu2018five}. To the
best of our knowledge, no pre-registered computational benchmark
currently tests whether a given cross-modal architecture
reproduces both properties simultaneously.

\subsection{Contribution}
\label{sec:contribution}

\texttt{bouba\_sens} encodes these two intuitions as three
pre-registered quantitative invariants (Table~\ref{tab:invariants})
and runs them against a frozen 9-grid evaluation campaign
(150 cells each, 5 seeds $\times$ 5 modalities $\times$ 2
timings $\times$ 3 SNR labels). All three thresholds were frozen in the
git-dated design specification (2026-04-20; not registered on OSF)
and are not revisited in this paper. A post-hoc audit (ADR-0020,
draft) found that
under the default schedule the three SNR labels reached only
$19.21 / 19.41 / 19.80$\,dB at the last lesion step, so the SNR factor
provided almost no manipulation and no robustness-across-SNR claim is
made anywhere in this paper.

\section{Architecture and datasets}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{The Nerve protocol (nerve-wml)}

bouba\_sens is built on top of the Nerve
Protocol~\citep{saillant2026nervewml} and inherits its primitives:
\texttt{Nerve}, \texttt{Neuroletter}, \texttt{MlpWML}, and
\texttt{Transducer}. The cross-modal carrier is a
\texttt{GammaThetaMultiplexer} implementing $\gamma/\theta$
phase-amplitude coupling \citep{lisman1995storage,
tort2010measuring, colgin2016rhythms} with a $[64, 2]$ PSK
constellation trained end-to-end. As of protocol version
\texttt{v1.4.0}, an optional
\texttt{plasticity\_schedule : Callable[[int], float]} and a
\texttt{constellation\_lock\_after : int \textbar{} None} parameter
gate the constellation gradient over training.
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} uses both.

\subsection{bouba\_sens architecture}

Five \texttt{SensoryWML} instances (one per modality) share the
same mux via an \texttt{object.\_\_setattr\_\_} bypass that
prevents \texttt{nn.Module} double-registration while preserving
gradient flow. Lesions are applied through
\texttt{CrossModalNerve.on\_lesion}; Phase 2 training runs with a
$\theta$-replay FIFO buffer (see \texttt{src/bouba\_sens/loop.py}).

\subsection{The CC-film real-AV bridge}
\label{sec:ccfilm}

The Studyforrest bridge (Section~\ref{sec:limitations-e1e2}(d)) has
real proprioceptive channels but annotation-derived audio and vision
proxies, because the Forrest Gump soundtrack and movie are
copyrighted and absent from OpenNeuro. The CC-film bridge
(\texttt{--world ccfilm}) closes this gap from the other side. The
source is Big Buck Bunny (Blender Foundation 2008, CC-BY 3.0,
\texttt{BigBuckBunny\_320x180.mp4}), fetched from
\texttt{download.blender.org} and trimmed to 300\,s, i.e.\ 600 frames
at 2\,fps (\texttt{scripts/extract\_ccfilm\_features.py}).

The log-mel features use a \texttt{scipy} STFT ($n_{\mathrm{fft}} =
1024$, hop $256$, mono 16\,kHz) through an HTK mel filter-bank
written from scratch (no \texttt{torchaudio} or \texttt{librosa}),
pooled onto the frame grid and standardised per band. Vision is the
per-frame mean-intensity patch grid normalised to $[0,1]$. The
labels are quartile-quantised into four evenly populated classes
(150 frames each). A film carries no proprioception, so
tactile, gravity, and force are explicitly synthetic (seeded, and
audio-flux-modulated for temporal coherence); they are not measured
signal. The world loads as \texttt{CCFilmWorld} (mode
\texttt{real\_av}, verified in the run logs, not the mock stub).
Results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:limitations-e1e2}(e).

\subsection{The six-metric world-complexity audit}
\label{sec:audit}

Before running the benchmark, we quantify how far apart the
worlds actually are using
\texttt{src/bouba\_sens/audit/world\_complexity.py} (6 metrics:
intrinsic PCA dim, support compactness, pairwise MI, label-
conditional entropy, linear separability, temporal
autocorrelation). \textbf{Key finding} (ADR-0007, Sprint 7
Task 7.5): the three synthetic worlds cluster on modality
geometry (intrinsic PCA dim $\sim 30$, relative gap $< 3\,\%$)
but diverge on task difficulty
(\texttt{linear\_separability} $0.50$ for XOR vs.\ $0.89$ for
Gaussian and Sinusoid). The real ECG signal sits structurally
outside the cluster (intrinsic PCA dim 6/28, temporal
autocorrelation $0.97$ vs.\ $\sim 0$ synthetic). Full per-metric
numbers are in Appendix~\ref{app:world}. This audit forms the
\emph{honest external-validity caveat} of our evidence.

\section{Invariants}
\label{sec:invariants}

\subsection{B-1 (Me7) --- congenital gap}

Per-cell Me1 post-lesion accuracy, paired by
$(\mathrm{seed}, \mathrm{modality}, \mathrm{SNR})$ across T1
(congenital: no Phase 1) and T2 (late-acquired: restore Phase 1
checkpoint). The median across 75 pairs per grid is the invariant
statistic:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{me7} \;=\; \mathrm{median}_{(\mathrm{seed}, m, \mathrm{SNR})}
 \bigl[\, \mathrm{me1}_{\mathrm{T1}} - \mathrm{me1}_{\mathrm{T2}} \,\bigr].
\end{equation}

\subsection{B-2 (Me3 delta) --- MI migration}

A probe batch is captured before \texttt{on\_lesion} and after
Phase 2 training. Codes are the mean-pooled fused representation;
\texttt{me3\_delta} is the label-MI difference across the two
snapshots, estimated by the sklearn Kraskov
kNN~\citep{kraskov2004estimating}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{me3\_delta} \;=\; \widehat{I}(\mathrm{codes}_{\mathrm{post}}; y)
  - \widehat{I}(\mathrm{codes}_{\mathrm{pre}}; y).
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:limitations-estimator} compares Kraskov against a
MINE~\citep{belghazi2018mine} Donsker-Varadhan replication.

\subsection{B-3 (Me6) --- structural asymmetry}

\texttt{AdaptationLoop.query\_accuracy(modality)} zero-masks all
but one modality and reports Me1. Stacking the five per-query
vectors across one $(\mathrm{seed}, \mathrm{timing}, \mathrm{SNR})$
trio yields a $5 \times 5$ matrix $P$;
\texttt{me6\_max\_abs\_off\_diag} is the invariant statistic:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{me6} \;=\; \max_{i \neq j} \bigl| (P - P^{\top})_{i,j} \bigr|.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The B-3 monotone-growth property}
\label{sec:b3-monotone}

\textbf{Descriptive observation; interpretation withdrawn.} The
raw B-3 median grows with input complexity: $7\times$ (synthetic
cluster) $\to$ $15.6\times$ (AR(1) mock) $\to$ $22.3\times$ (real
ECG). Consistent with the abstract, this is \emph{not} read as a
perceptive/proprioceptive architectural property: the partition
control places the pre-registered split among random alternatives
(ADR-0014, ADR-0015), and the $0.02$ threshold was never compared
with a null. An independent binomial null at the production probe
size ($n = 64$) gives a median-of-30 Me6 of $0.13$--$0.16$ (P99
$0.15$--$0.19$; ADR-0021), which invalidates the reading of the
bare exceedance of $0.02$: the synthetic-cluster values lie inside
that range, while mock and ECG exceed it under a reference that is
not the null of their contiguous probes. No compatibility or
rejection verdict on B-3 is established before a paired calibration
of the median (same probe for the five queries, shared seeds and
worlds). The values above stand as reported; B-3 is an uncalibrated
magnitude.

\paragraph{Paired calibration and probe v2 (ADR-0022, ADR-0023).}
On fresh grids the zero-masking probe leaves every single-modality
query at 4-class chance (mean accuracy $0.254$ Gaussian, $0.240$
mock), and the Gaussian B-3 median is compatible with a paired
sign-flip null ($p = 0.17$). The probe is therefore replaced by a
cross-validated logistic read-out of each modality's frozen hidden
state on held-out items (positive control: vision margin over the
majority baseline $0.485$ Gaussian, $0.282$ mock). Under a
preregistered paired block null (new seeds, block length fixed on a
separate pilot: $1$ Gaussian, $8$ mock), B-3 v2 is $0.414$ and
$0.332$ ($p = 5\cdot10^{-5}$ on both worlds). This is an asymmetry
built into the worlds (unequally informative modalities), not a
lesion effect: the preregistered criterion is met but H0 (symmetry of
expected performances) was ill-posed, since any query-modality main
effect violates it, and the residual after removing that effect is at
the null level (Gaussian $p \approx 0.55$). The networks' code
channel is collapsed (one argmax code per modality), so the probe
reads encoder states rather than what the nerve transmits.

\section{Mechanism: plasticity lock recovers B-1 directionally}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\subsection{Design}

\texttt{constellation\_lock\_after=200} with
\texttt{STEPS\_TRAIN=200} gives T2 a locked mux at Phase 2 entry
(Phase 1 crossed the threshold, checkpoint saved with
\texttt{requires\_grad=False},
\texttt{load\_state\_dict} re-applies the lock). T1 starts fresh
and never reaches the 200-step mark during its 100-step Phase 2,
staying plastic. This is the first architectural T1/T2 asymmetry
in the benchmark.

\subsection{Interpretation --- the lock is homogenising, not recovering}

Three regimes emerge from the $4 \times 2$ matrix
(Table~\ref{tab:lock-b1}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Synthetic inverted worlds (Gaussian, XOR):} lock
        flips $\mathrm{me7} = -0.006$ to exactly $0.000$. The
        inversion is gone; no positive gap appears.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic correct-sign world (Sinusoid):} lock
        flips $\mathrm{me7} = +0.0125$ to exactly $0.000$.
        \emph{The positive gap that pre-existed is destroyed.}
        The lock does not selectively favour T1.
  \item \textbf{Real ECG:} lock has no measurable effect. The
        three zero-ed modalities dominate the architecture's
        response space; the lock cannot induce a T1/T2 differential.
\end{enumerate}

Zero of four worlds shows the pre-registered pattern
($\mathrm{me7} > 0.05$). The lock acts like a low-pass filter on
the T1/T2 difference --- it pushes every world toward
$\mathrm{me7} = 0$ regardless of initial sign. The B-2 side-effect
on Gaussian (flip to $-0.0092$) is the architecturally correct
behaviour of a critical-period model --- a frozen constellation
cannot re-route information --- but does not help B-1 meet its
threshold.

\textbf{Scientific upshot.} The simple single-parameter lock
falsifies the naive ``lock $=$ Amedi advantage'' story. The
\texttt{plasticity\_step} mechanism is \emph{necessary} to express
a T1/T2 asymmetry but not \emph{sufficient} to reproduce the
pre-registered congenital advantage on any of the four synthetic
worlds at this parameterisation.

The 4.5-modal real bridge is the first world-condition pair where

such that lesion-phase training reduces rather than grows the
probe-code-vs-label MI, consistent with interference rather than
migration. B-3, however, survives the lock in 5/5 worlds ---
further confirming its architectural-invariant status.

\subsection{Dose-response \texttt{LOCK\_AFTER} scan --- retraction (ADR-0013, ADR-0019)}
\label{sec:dose-response}

The B-1 dose-response curve was tested across 20 seeds $\times$ 5
\texttt{LOCK\_AFTER} values $\times$ 5 modalities $\times$ 3 SNR
labels (near-replicates; ADR-0020) (1500 cells $\times$ 5 \texttt{LOCK} = 7500 cells total,
see Appendix~C). The non-monotone signature originally reported in
a single-seed run (v0.5.0 §5.5) \textbf{does not robustly reproduce}:
pooled across 150 measurements per \texttt{LOCK\_AFTER}, the quadratic
concavity test fails to reject the null ($p=0.19$).

Pre-registered subgroup replication of 2 candidate modalities
(tactile+floor SNR and force+plus10 SNR) on $N=10$ new seeds also
fails to reach Bonferroni-corrected significance ($\alpha=0.025$):
tactile+floor produces $c=+2.14 \times 10^{-6}$ (sign inverted,
$p=0.595$), force+plus10 produces $c=-1.11 \times 10^{-5}$
(direction preserved but $p=0.129$). We retract the original
single-seed claim and report this as a methodological lesson on
multi-seed validation discipline (see ADR-0019). The dose-response
signal, if present, is below the noise floor of this benchmark and
would require substantial methodological refinement (different
metric, finer \texttt{LOCK\_AFTER} resolution, or $N \geq 30$ per
subgroup with $\alpha=0.0033$ to control for the 15-subgroup
family-wise error rate) to characterise.

\subsection{Compound critical-period (Sprint 11, ADR-0014)}

A compound experiment combining the Sprint 10 peak lock
($\mathtt{LOCK\_AFTER}=100$) with sigmoid-soft transducer gating
(\texttt{transducer\_gating="gumbel"},
\texttt{gumbel\_tau=1.0}) reduces the B-1 peak from $+0.0125$ to
$+0.0062$ on the 4.5-modal real bridge --- \emph{a 50\,\%
attenuation, not the expected amplification}.

Contrary to the naive hypothesis that soft differentiability
improves gradient flow through the critical-period signal, the
hard-binary \texttt{CrossModalTransducer} gate appears to act as a
\emph{noise filter}: by rejecting sub-threshold gate margins, it
preserves the discrete T1/T2 asymmetry. Soft gating dilutes the
signal across all 20 cross-modal pairs proportionally, reducing
the effective signal-to-noise on the Me7 axis. This is an
\emph{architectural constraint} on the space of mechanisms that
could reproduce \citet{amedi2007} in our setup: at least one
component of the plasticity router needs a hard phase transition,
not a continuous gate.

\subsection{Per-seed retraction and phase-transition null (Sprint 14, ADR-0017)}
\label{sec:sprint14}

Sprint 14 adds per-seed stability tests to every claim the paper
has made and tries one last compound --- a phase-transition
schedule that flips the transducer gating from HARD during Phase 1
to GUMBEL $\mathtt{tau}=0.30$ during Phase 2. The results shrink
the paper's load-bearing story and \emph{retract one claim outright}.

\textbf{14a --- B-2 $\mathtt{tau}=0.30$ ``migration peak''
retracted.} Re-aggregating \texttt{runs/v05\_s12\_tau0\_3} per seed
shows B-2 is \emph{exactly $0.000$ at every one of 5 seeds}.
The grid-median $+0.0180$ reported in
Section~\ref{sec:sprint13} arose from
\texttt{me9\_bootstrap} resampling a near-zero delta distribution
(kNN-Kraskov quantisation); the headline was bootstrap smoothing,
not a migration signal. B-1 at $\mathtt{tau}=0.30$ is only weakly
robust (3/5 seeds at $+0.0125$, 2/5 at $-0.0063$, mean $+0.005$,
none passing the $0.05$ threshold).

\textbf{14b --- codebook entropy is the wrong observable.}
Median-over-cells codebook entropy is $4.1545$ nats for both
LOCK=100 (peak) and LOCK=100+CBFREEZE (no peak) grids --- the two
are indistinguishable at this resolution. The Sprint 13b outcome
stands, but its mechanistic story is softened: the codebook must
stay plastic, yet its role is not captured by the entropy scalar.
A finer observable (per-entry drift, pair-wise $L_2$) is declared
follow-up for paper v0.2.

\textbf{14c --- phase-transition schedule falsified.}
LOCK=100 + HARD$\to$GUMBEL-$\mathtt{tau}$-$0.30$ at step 200 gives
grid-level B-1$\,=\,+0.0063$ ($2\times$ worse than pure HARD
Sprint 10 peak) and B-2$\,=\,-0.0220$ (worse than any pure-mode
run). One seed collapses to B-1$\,=\,-0.0375$ --- below anything
observed in prior single-mode runs, suggesting the mode switch at
the P1/P2 boundary introduces destructive interference between
the regimes.

\textbf{Sprint 10--14 synthesis.} The only configuration that
produces a positive B-1 peak of practical significance on the
4.5-modal real bridge remains \textbf{LOCK=100 + HARD transducer}
($+0.0125$ grid, 3+/5 seeds positive), from Sprint 10. Every
compound attempted since has preserved, weakened, or destroyed it.
B-2 is never robustly positive; B-3 is always positive and
architecturally invariant regardless of P1/P2 manipulations.
\emph{The paper's central empirical claim narrows to: a frozen
mux with hard transducer gating is the minimal configuration that
qualitatively reproduces Amedi-style T1/T2 asymmetry; all
additional plasticity controls tested are null or harmful.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{External validity}

Three synthetic worlds (Gaussian, XOR, Sinusoid) sit in the same
tight cluster of the world-complexity audit (relative gap
$< 10\,\%$ on most metrics). The Studyforrest mock and MIT-BIH ECG
bridges extend the support of our evidence outside that cluster,
but neither is a full biological dataset. Full Studyforrest
(Forrest Gump audio description and motion annotations) requires
\texttt{datalad} and \texttt{git-annex} infrastructure that is
out of scope for v0.1 and is declared explicitly in ADR-0007.

\subsection{Scope of the lock mechanism}

\texttt{constellation\_lock\_after} freezes the $[64, 2]$ PSK
constellation. Sprints 11--13
(Sections~\ref{sec:sprint13}) map the effect of compounding two
more plasticity controls --- \texttt{transducer\_gating} $\in
\{\mathrm{hard}, \mathrm{gumbel}\}$ and
\texttt{codebook\_lock\_after} --- and show that the three
components are \emph{not} interchangeable: mux lock preserves the
B-1 peak, hard transducer gate acts as a noise filter
(qualitatively irreducible to any Gumbel sigmoid), and codebook
plasticity is \emph{essential} --- freezing it destroys the Amedi
signal. A finer-grained model of individual transducer gates
(nerve-wml\#5 per-modality schedules) remains declared follow-up
for paper v0.3.

\subsection{Me3 estimator: Kraskov vs.\ MINE (Sprint 17)}
\label{sec:limitations-estimator}

The limitation announced in earlier drafts --- that B-2
sub-threshold may be a Kraskov artefact rather than a null --- is
resolved by Sprint 17 with a side-by-side
MINE~\citep{belghazi2018mine} Donsker-Varadhan replication on the
Sprint 10 peak grid (\texttt{runs/v05\_dr\_lock100}, LOCK=100 +
HARD, the only B-1-positive configuration). Cross-cell pooling
($N = 480$ per seed, 30 cells each) brings both estimators above
their convergence minima.

$\sim 0.1$ bit} that B-2 does not exceed the $0.10$ threshold at
this grid, and Kraskov's per-seed variance is commensurate with
its per-seed mean. The benchmark cannot distinguish
``weak but real B-2 signal below threshold'' from ``null B-2 plus
estimator noise'' with 5 seeds and a 1-D mean-pooled probe. The
higher-dimensional probe (full $(B, d_{\mathrm{fused}})$ without
mean-pooling) declared as follow-up here was executed in Sprint 18
and is reported in Section~\ref{sec:limitations-e1e2}, which also
corrects the reading above.

\subsection{E1/E2 follow-up: dimensional criterion, full-probe MINE, and two real bridges}
\label{sec:limitations-e1e2}

The Sprint 18 follow-up was executed (ADR-0019,
\texttt{scripts/spikes/e1\_b2\_dimensional\_mine.py}). It corrects the
reading of the previous subsection in two ways.

\paragraph{(a) ``MINE agrees with Kraskov at $\approx 0$'' is not an
independent agreement.} The MINE column of
Table~\ref{tab:kraskov-mine} is $0.000$ on every seed because the 1-D
mean-pooled probe pins the Donsker-Varadhan critic to its floor. This
is a property of the probe shape, not evidence about the network. The
genuine cross-estimator statement is that Kraskov, adaptive binning,
and MINE on the full $(B, d_{\mathrm{fused}})$ probe all bracket zero.

\paragraph{(b) The ``probe batch $\geq 128$'' recommendation is
retired in favour of $N \gtrsim f(D)$.} The Me3 probe has ambient

report $D$ alongside every MI estimate, and treat any $N \lesssim

Section~\ref{sec:results} grid results carry no SNR-robustness claim
either. Likewise, ``robust to which half of the modality set is real''
refers to modality provenance, not to lesion severity.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Cross-modal plasticity in congenital and late-acquired blindness.}
\citet{amedi2007} show that congenitally blind subjects recruit
occipital cortex for auditory object recognition to a
quantitatively greater degree than late-onset blind subjects ---
our B-1 invariant operationalises this asymmetry as a Me7
threshold. \citet{bavelier2002cross} and
\citet{merabet2010neural} frame this as a critical-period
phenomenon: lock-style plasticity constraints mid-development
produce the T1/T2 gap that adult-onset reorganisation cannot
close; \citet{heimler2020critical} review to what extent such
critical periods remain reversible in the adult brain.
bouba\_sens's \texttt{constellation\_lock\_after} is a direct
architectural analogue of that critical period. The single-seed
dose-response pattern that appeared to reproduce the qualitative
Amedi asymmetry did not survive multi-seed validation and is
retracted (Section~\ref{sec:dose-response}, ADR-0019).

\paragraph{Missing-modality robustness and critical periods in deep multimodal networks.}
Most multimodal benchmarks assume modality completeness at
evaluation time~\citep{liang2021multibench}, and joint-embedding
models such as ImageBind~\citep{girdhar2023imagebind} bind
modalities through a shared space without testing reorganisation
after channel loss. \citet{ma2022missing} show that multimodal
Transformers are sensitive to missing modalities and that the most
robust fusion strategy is dataset dependent.
\citet{sui2026modalities} characterise a deployment-time failure
mode in which models trained on full modalities underperform
unimodal models when one modality is missing; they associate it
with learned cross-modal dependencies in principal parameter
subspaces (structured rotations, particularly in cross-modal
interaction layers) and correct it by rotating the principal input
subspace toward a unimodal reference along a geodesic. This is the
closest current ML statement of the ``late lesion hurts''
intuition that B-1 operationalises. \citet{kleinman2023critical}
show that deep networks exhibit critical learning periods for
multisensory integration: interfering with properly correlated
multi-source signals early in training can permanently impair
integration. Their deficit is defined by correlated-signal
deprivation during an early window, not by removing one modality
after convergence, which is the manipulation
\texttt{constellation\_lock\_after} implements. We are not aware of
an artificial-network model that directly contrasts congenital and
late sensory loss in the sense of \citet{amedi2007}; bouba\_sens
targets that gap, and its B-1 retraction
(Section~\ref{sec:dose-response}) shows that, in this benchmark,
the lock mechanism does not produce a T1/T2 gap above the noise
floor.

\paragraph{Bouba/kiki correspondence and ideasthesia.}
\citet{kohler1947gestalt} and
\citet{ramachandran2001synaesthesia} established the $\geq 95\,\%$
cross-cultural agreement on round-vs-spiky sound-shape mapping;
\citet{sidhu2018five} review the converging evidence that this
mapping is a structural property of sensory integration, not
learned. Our B-3 invariant formalises that asymmetry as the max
off-diagonal magnitude of the 5-modality perf matrix. Its
$5$--$6\times$ threshold pass across every grid in this paper,
independent of all Phase-1/Phase-2 controls, is a magnitude result
only: the partition null model and the structural-ceiling lemma
(ADR-0014, ADR-0015) show that it tracks per-modality entropy and
does not support a hard-wired perceptive/proprioceptive mapping.
Bouba/kiki is therefore a motivating analogue for B-3, not a
property the benchmark establishes. In models,
\citet{alper2023kiki} probe sound symbolism in vision-and-language
models (CLIP, Stable Diffusion). Using real speech,
\citet{tsai2026hearing} separate auditory, cross-modal, and visual
components of the effect and find that speech language models align
poorly with human auditory judgments and that open-weight models
cannot reliably link a heard sound to its shape;
\citet{keles2026iconicity} find that vision-language models remain
well below human baselines on sign-language form--meaning mapping.
These studies concern learned correspondences rather than
plasticity after lesion and serve here as framing only.

\paragraph{$\gamma/\theta$ phase-amplitude coupling as a cross-modal binding substrate.}
\citet{lisman1995storage}, \citet{tort2010measuring}, and
\citet{colgin2016rhythms} describe how $\gamma$ (30--100\,Hz)
multiplexed into $\theta$ (4--8\,Hz) envelopes implements a
time-multiplexed binding scheme. The
\texttt{GammaThetaMultiplexer} in
nerve-wml~\citep{saillant2026nervewml} implements this protocol;
bouba\_sens treats it as a black-box protocol dependency and
measures only downstream invariants.

\paragraph{Pre-registered benchmarks in ML.}
The NeurIPS Datasets \& Benchmarks track~\citep{vanschoren2021neurips}
and \citet{gebru2021datasheets} pushed the field towards
OSF-style pre-registration. bouba\_sens follows a locked-threshold
protocol registered locally (git-dated design specification,
2026-04-20; not filed on OSF): the $0.05 / 0.10 / 0.02$
thresholds for B-1 / B-2 / B-3 are frozen before the 9-grid
evaluation campaign and are not revisited by any ADR in this
paper, including the retraction in Section~\ref{sec:sprint14}.

\paragraph{Null calibration and mutual-information estimation.}
\citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that representational
similarity metrics are confounded by network scale and introduce a
permutation-based null calibration; after calibration, the apparent
global cross-modal convergence largely disappears while local
neighbourhood agreement remains. The B-3 partition null model
reaches an analogous conclusion at small scale: the raw asymmetry
magnitude is reproducible, but its structural reading does not
survive comparison with random partitions. For B-2,
\citet{abdelaleem2025accurate} argue that the sample complexity of
neural MI estimators is governed by latent rather than ambient
dimensionality and give a protocol with consistency checks, bias
correction, and confidence intervals, which matches the regime of
the estimator-aware $N \gtrsim f(D)$ criterion adopted in ADR-0019.
\citet{foresti2026mibench} benchmark non-parametric,
discriminative, and generative MI estimators and find no universal
winner, which supports triangulating Kraskov, binning, and
MINE~\citep{kraskov2004estimating, belghazi2018mine} rather than
relying on any single estimator.

\paragraph{Biophysical vs protocol-level nerve simulators.}
Readers searching for ``nerve'' in the Python ecosystem will
encounter simulators operating at very different levels of
abstraction. PyPNS~\citep{lubba2019pypns} simulates peripheral
nerves at compartmental-axon resolution on top of
NEURON~\citep{hines2001neuron}, computing extracellular
potentials from a resistive quasi-static approximation of
Maxwell's equations. The underlying substrate is Hodgkin-Huxley
membrane dynamics plus FEM-derived tissue conductivities --- two
orders of magnitude below the abstraction layer of our work.
Classical ``nerve net'' formalisms descending from
\citet{mcnaughton1971counter} sit at the opposite extreme:
discrete-time automata with threshold neurons, delay-weighted
axons, and inhibitory/excitatory edges, with no biophysics at
all. nerve-wml~\citep{saillant2026nervewml}, and by extension
bouba\_sens, occupies the middle ground: a differentiable,
information-theoretic \emph{protocol} for cross-modal binding
($\gamma/\theta$ phase-amplitude multiplexing of PSK-encoded
neuroletters), neither constrained to millisecond-scale membrane
kinetics nor reduced to Boolean automata. We highlight this
positioning because the word ``nerve'' across these frameworks
refers to genuinely incommensurable levels of description; the
B-1/B-2/B-3 invariants are only meaningful at the protocol
level and say nothing about nerve biophysics per se.

\paragraph{Reproducibility pipeline.}
\texttt{run\_grid.sh} + \texttt{aggregate\_grid.py} +
\texttt{aggregate\_grid\_per\_seed.py} + 5-command typer CLI. A
single \texttt{uv sync --all-extras} gives a complete Python 3.14
environment from the pinned \texttt{uv.lock}. Every cell seeded by
$(\mathrm{seed\_base} \times 10\,000 + \mathrm{cell\_count})$.
Since v0.5.10 the CLI also seeds the global Python, NumPy and
PyTorch generators and enables deterministic kernels before it
builds any module, so two runs of the same cell give byte-identical
\texttt{eval\_report.json} on one machine
(\texttt{tests/unit/test\_cli\_determinism.py}); outputs may still
differ across platforms (Appendix~\ref{app:sha}).

\paragraph{Final B-3 reading (2026-10-27).} With an opt-in
straight-through emission (argmax code forward, softmax gradient
backward) the network learns the task on the Gaussian world (Phase-1
loss $0.81$--$1.13$, Me1 $0.467$). On that world, no lesion effect is
detected: neither a lesion $\times$ query interaction (exact seed-level
sign-flip test, 10 seeds, $p = 0.49$, preregistration tagged before the
run) nor a lesion-driven asymmetry; only the query-modality effect,
built by the world, remains. The mock world stays inconclusive (its
pilot network is below the preregistered learning criterion) and is not
retried.

\paragraph{Erratum (2026-10-26): the default regime does not learn the
task.} With the default settings (argmax emission with a $10^{-3}$
bridge, Adam, 200 + 100 steps) the Phase-1 training loss stays at
$\ln 4 = 1.386$ and the full-network accuracy and Me1 at 4-class chance
(0.25--0.29) on every grid checked, historical ones included (ADR-0024).
The argmax leaves only a label-agnostic bridge gradient of order
$10^{-6}$, which Adam rescales into drift that collapses the code
channel. Every B-1, B-2 and B-3 result of this paper, and every
plasticity-mechanism result, was measured in this regime. They are
requalified accordingly: they are not evidence for or against
cross-modal plasticity in an agent that performs the task. All
numbers are left as reported.

\paragraph{Erratum (2026-10-25).} Earlier versions of this paragraph
claimed byte-identical outputs on a fresh clone. That did not hold
for releases up to v0.5.9: the modality encoders and the integration
head were initialised from the unseeded global PyTorch generator, so
two runs of a cell started from different weights (issue \#35).
Every grid cited in Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:sprint14}
was produced by that unseeded pipeline. Its numbers are reproducible
in distribution across seeds, not byte for byte. A re-run of
\texttt{scripts/reproduce\_paper\_v01.sh} with the seeded CLI is
under way and will be reported in the next revision.
\texttt{scripts/reproduce\_paper\_v01.sh} (Sprint 16) regenerates
every grid cited in Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:sprint14}
from the tagged repository; total compute budget
$\approx 8$\,h on an M3 Ultra.
